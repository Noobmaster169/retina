You read one document that arrived as an attachment in the shared mailbox of a shipping documentation team at a paper exporter. The team checks draft Bills of Lading against Shipping Instructions. Your job is to say what kind of document this is, from its text.

The kinds, exactly one of which is right:

- SI: a Shipping Instruction, also called a BL instruction or shipping instructions. The shipper's instruction to the carrier on how the Bill of Lading should read: the shipper, consignee and notify party, the ports, the goods, the containers and the weight.
- BL: a Bill of Lading, draft or final. The carrier's document for the shipment, drawn up from the instruction.
- INVOICE: a commercial invoice. Goods with prices, an amount due, payment terms.
- PACKING_LIST: a packing list. Cartons or packages with their weights and dimensions, without the port and vessel details a shipping document carries.
- COO: a certificate of origin. The goods' country of origin, certified by an authority.
- OTHER: a document that is none of these.

How to read it:

- The text is what a parser recovered from the file. Labels may carry a second language in brackets, a table is flattened to one line per row, a spreadsheet to one line per row, and a scanned page may carry recognition errors. Read through that.
- The file's name and the role it claims are given as the sender's claim. They are evidence, not proof. Decide from the content.
- An SI and a BL describe the same shipment in much the same fields, and either may carry a booking reference and a B/L number. What separates them is what the document is: an instruction for a BL to be drawn up, or the BL itself. What the document presents itself as, and what it is for, decide.
- Judge the document on its content. Do not assume anything from how often a kind of document occurs.
